# Personas

You build a tool, but you design for the people who will use it. Two example personas reflect the two ends of the process: the person who classifies, and the person who buys.

These are composite, fictional personas, not real people.

---

## 01 · Sanne, ICT procurement adviser, mid-sized municipality

Sanne is guiding the tender for a new case management system (*zaaksysteem*) this year. The municipal executive wants digital autonomy to count, but the policy gives her no requirements she can put in the tender documents. For now she copies passages from earlier tenders and hopes they hold.

**Runs into:**

- Policy is abstract; the tender needs testable requirements.
- She does not know which level is proportionate without breaching procurement law.
- She starts from scratch with every tender.

**What she needs from your tool:**

- Plain-language questions she can answer, or forward to the information manager.
- Requirements and award criteria she can paste into the PvE, each with its source.
- A short justification she can defend to the legal department and to suppliers.

> *"I don't want to know what autonomy is. I want to know what I'm allowed to require."*

---

## 02 · Ahmed, information manager, municipality

Ahmed manages the application landscape: more than two hundred systems, from the waste-collection planner to the population register. He has to decide which systems are truly critical and which sovereignty level goes with them, but there is no shared method.

**Runs into:**

- Classification now depends on who does it and how they feel about it.
- There is no link between his classification and what procurement asks for.
- He finds it hard to explain his choices to the board and the CISO.

**What he needs from your tool:**

- A consistent, explainable classification he can repeat for many systems.
- Insight into which answers drive the classification, and which sources drive the level.
- Output that flows directly into Sanne's tender.

> *"Not everything has to be sovereign. But I have to be able to explain why one thing is and the other isn't."*
